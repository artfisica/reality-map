# The Civilian Geopolitical Reality Map

## How to Understand States by What They Do, Not What They Declare

**By Arturo Sánchez Pineda**  
**20 August 2026**

## Author’s position

I write as a Venezuelan living in Switzerland and as a physicist and engineer, trained to separate observation, assumption, uncertainty and conclusion. My residence outside Venezuela gives me neither neutrality nor permission to ignore what is happening. My scientific training does not make my values disappear; it obliges me to show where the evidence ends and my interpretation begins.

This manual is not a theory that law is irrelevant. It begins from the opposite premise: law supplies the vocabulary by which force can be identified, rights can be claimed and responsibility can be recorded. But a rule on paper is not an enforcement mechanism. Civilians who confuse legal entitlement with practical protection misunderstand both their risks and the institutions on which they rely.

The purpose of this map is therefore practical. It explains how to compare what states say with what they do; how to recognise coercion hidden beneath administrative or diplomatic language; how to separate evidence from prediction; and how to make personal decisions without treating official assurances as guarantees.

The personal origin of this project is recorded in two earlier texts, *Miedo* and *Dolor*. The first was written in December 2025, while US forces were killing people on boats near Venezuela and before the January intervention. The second was written after the feared expansion of force had occurred. They are not legal authorities and will never be used as proof of an external event. They establish why I ask these questions and why an antiseptic vocabulary would be dishonest to the people who bear the consequences.

I do not write from the comfortable position of someone who declares himself braver or morally purer than everyone else. In *Miedo*, I included myself in the accusation: “yes, of course, me too.” In *Dolor*, I rejected the cheap gesture of “I told you so.” Those two decisions define the voice of this manual. Distance may give me physical safety; it does not grant me innocence or moral superiority. My task is to convert fear, discomfort and pain into questions that can be examined, challenged and corrected.

## Objectivity is not neutrality

Politics is not particle physics. States cannot be isolated in a detector, historical events cannot be repeated under controlled conditions and human intention is not directly measurable. A serious method must therefore be modest about causation. It should identify observable acts, state its assumptions, compare rival explanations, describe missing information and specify what evidence would weaken its conclusion.

That is objectivity. Neutrality is something else. It is a political position that can place the violator and the violated at an artificial equal distance from the observer. I will apply the same evidentiary standard to the United States, Israel, Russia, Venezuela and every other actor examined here. I will not pretend that applying the same standard requires the same judgment when their conduct is different.

## 1. The discipline of naming facts

Forceful writing does not require careless writing. Every major proposition in this project should be labelled, internally or explicitly, as one of the following:

| Category | Meaning | Permitted form |
|---|---|---|
| Verified event | A dated act supported by reliable evidence | “The United States used military force in Venezuela on 3 January 2026.” |
| Official position | What a government or institution says | “Venezuela stated that its ICC withdrawal responded to selectivity and politicisation.” |
| Investigative finding | A conclusion by a competent inquiry, not necessarily a court | “A UN commission concluded that genocide had been committed in Gaza.” |
| Judicial order or finding | The exact procedural and legal result reached by a court | “The ICJ ordered provisional measures; a final merits judgment remains pending.” |
| Allegation | A claim not yet judicially established | “The ICC warrants concern alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity.” |
| Analytical inference | A conclusion drawn from facts, clearly identified as analysis | “The sequence is consistent with a government acting under severe external coercion.” |
| Forecast or risk | A possible future outcome | “Venezuela faces a risk of prolonged institutional fragmentation.” |

This discipline prevents two common failures. The first is official euphemism: calling an attack an “operation,” domination a “status,” or killing an “interdiction.” The second is rhetorical overreach: treating suspicion as proof, pressure as legal occupation, or an arrest warrant as a conviction. The first hides power; the second gives powerful actors an easy reason to dismiss the whole argument.

Every analytical conclusion must also remain revisable. A claim that cannot be weakened by contrary evidence is not analysis; it is commitment. Revision does not mean moral retreat. It means that the force of the language must remain proportional to the force of the record.

## 2. The six-layer power map

For any state action, ask six questions.

1. **The rule:** What do international and domestic law require, permit or prohibit?
2. **The declaration:** Which principles does the state publicly invoke?
3. **The interest:** Which security, economic, electoral or institutional objective does it protect?
4. **The dependency:** Which alliances, currencies, technologies, trade routes, energy supplies or protecting powers restrict its choices?
5. **The instrument:** Which tools—force, sanctions, finance, trade, weapons, courts, diplomacy or aid—does it use or withhold?
6. **The cost:** Who receives the benefit and who is made to absorb the consequences?

The first two layers describe the legitimate order. The final four help predict conduct. The gap between them is the central object of this study.

A useful comparison begins with capability. If a government freezes assets, restricts trade, blocks technology and accepts domestic economic costs against one violator, it has demonstrated an administrative capacity. If it later answers another grave violation only with statements and humanitarian funding, “helplessness” is not a sufficient explanation. The identity of the violator, the protecting alliance and the domestic price of confrontation must be examined.

## 3. The enforcement gradient

States rarely choose simply between action and inaction. They select a level on an enforcement gradient:

1. verbal concern;
2. humanitarian assistance;
3. evidence collection and investigation;
4. suspension of cooperation or preferences;
5. targeted financial and travel measures;
6. arms restrictions or embargo;
7. broader economic and diplomatic isolation;
8. lawful collective enforcement.

The position selected matters more than the intensity of the language surrounding it. Humanitarian aid can save lives, but it can also socialise the cost of violence: donors finance food, shelter and reconstruction while preserving relations with the actor producing the need. This is **humanitarian substitution** when relief replaces available pressure rather than accompanying it.

Lebanon illustrates **recognition without consequence**. European and Swiss authorities condemned attacks, funded relief, supported the Lebanese Armed Forces and UN peacekeeping, and demanded respect for sovereignty. Those acts were real. Yet their response stopped below the level of coercive measures capable of changing the attacking state’s incentives. The accurate description is not “they did nothing.” It is **containment without deterrence**.

### Fiscal externalisation

Withholding aid is also an instrument. A government engages in **fiscal externalisation** when it keeps the domestic political benefit of a budget decision while transferring the bodily cost to people outside its electorate and outside the institutions capable of reversing it. The test requires four separations: debt from annual deficit; programme terminations from budget reductions; observed deaths from modelled excess mortality; and political or moral responsibility from a breach of a specific legal duty.

This distinction is developed in [Case Study VI](../studies/externalisation/). A severe debt position can create pressure without determining which expenditure is cut. A humanitarian programme may be voluntary while an assessed UN contribution, a duty to facilitate relief during armed conflict or the duty to prevent genocide follows a different legal rule. The map should identify the choice and its foreseeable consequences without claiming that every cruel cut is automatically a war crime.

## 4. The protected-ally test

Territorial size is a poor measure of a state’s effective geopolitical weight. Ask instead:

- Which major power guarantees its security?
- Is it integrated into intelligence, defence, technology or financial networks?
- Can pressure against it impose costs on the protecting power?
- Do domestic political coalitions in other states raise the price of confrontation?
- Are institutions structured to require consensus that allies can block?

This explains how a small country can constrain much larger European economies. The relevant actor is not the small state in isolation but the network within which it is protected. In the case of Israel, serious European pressure also means confronting a state embedded in an American-led security and political system. That does not remove European agency. It explains why governments often choose subordination over enforcement.

## 5. The colonial-continuity test

Not every dependent territory is legally occupied, and not every formally incorporated territory has completed decolonisation. The correct analysis follows a historical and institutional chain:

**conquest or annexation → metropolitan law → administrative normalisation → strategic and economic dependency → the present conditions of exit**

For each territory, examine:

1. How was it acquired?
2. What is its current legal classification?
3. Where does final sovereign authority reside?
4. What citizenship and national representation exist?
5. Is it listed by the United Nations as a Non-Self-Governing Territory?
6. What military or strategic function does it perform?
7. How dependent is it on metropolitan finance, trade and administration?
8. Is there a credible and locally controlled path to alter its status?
9. What coercion surrounds the present arrangement?
10. What does this mean for civilians in practice?

Puerto Rico and Guam entered United States sovereignty through the 1898 war and treaty with Spain. Puerto Rico is an unincorporated US territory whose relationship remains subject to congressional authority. Guam is also an unincorporated territory and remains on the [UN list of Non-Self-Governing Territories](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n22/749/47/pdf/n2274947.pdf). Neither should be casually described as a present belligerent occupation; both display the conversion of military acquisition into durable legal administration.

France annexed New Caledonia in 1853. It is a sui generis collectivity and a UN-listed Non-Self-Governing Territory whose institutional future remains contested. French Guiana is different: it is constitutionally integrated into France under Article 73, with French and European citizenship and national representation, while remaining marked by the material dependencies and inequalities of colonial incorporation. That integration is differentiated: [Article 138 of the Convention implementing the Schengen Agreement](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000000165861) limits its application for France to the European territory of the Republic, leaving French Guiana outside Schengen. Formal citizenship changes the legal analysis; it does not make history or structural dependency disappear.

Venezuela does not belong in this territorial category. It is a sovereign UN member subjected in 2026 to unlawful foreign military force and continuing coercion. Conflating that intervention with legal occupation would obscure the applicable test: occupation requires effective control over territory, and the Swiss government’s legal assessment found that threshold had not been met at the time of its memorandum.

## 6. The intervention lifecycle

External interventions are sold at the moment of entry and judged domestically at the moment of exit. Civilians live through the entire lifecycle:

1. **Target construction:** a foreign government or group is defined as an exceptional threat.
2. **Legal compression:** disputed doctrines or secret classifications replace ordinary restraints.
3. **Overwhelming entry:** superior military power produces rapid visible results.
4. **Institutional breakage:** state capacity, infrastructure and chains of authority are damaged or dissolved.
5. **Fragmentation:** armed groups, external patrons and informal economies fill the vacuum.
6. **Civilian transfer:** death, displacement, disability, lost education and economic collapse are absorbed locally.
7. **Narrative revision:** stated goals narrow as costs rise.
8. **External exit:** the intervening power declares an end, reduces exposure or changes priorities.
9. **Long aftermath:** the population continues living with insecurity and weakened institutions.

Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria are not identical and must not be assigned a single cause. Iraq followed a US-led invasion whose official British inquiry found was launched before peaceful options were exhausted and without adequate post-conflict preparation. Afghanistan combined an initial response to the 11 September attacks with a two-decade state-building project that the US Special Inspector General concluded was undermined by unrealistic timelines and poor understanding of local institutions. Syria’s devastation arose cumulatively from state repression, civil war, ISIS, Russian and Iranian intervention, US-led operations, Turkish action and multiple proxy structures.

The common civilian lesson is narrower and stronger: whatever limited institutional or infrastructure gains occurred, these interventions did not produce the stable, secure and democratic societies used to justify them. They left mass displacement, damaged institutions and fragmented sovereignty, while the intervening powers retained far greater control over the timing and meaning of their withdrawal.

Wars end for an intervening power when its government says the mission has ended. They do not end for the population that inherits the consequences.

## 7. The target-construction test

The United States’ maritime strikes beginning in September 2025 show how executive classification can replace arrest and trial. Official US oversight reports state that dozens of small vessels were destroyed and more than two hundred people killed or presumed dead through June 2026. The administration described targets through counterterrorism and armed-conflict categories, while Congress had not specifically authorised force against drug-trafficking organisations and the full target list, evidence and mission statement remained classified.

For every claimed lethal target, ask:

1. Who made the designation?
2. What evidence is public?
3. Does an armed conflict legally exist?
4. Was there an imminent threat to life?
5. Was capture or non-lethal interdiction feasible?
6. Was each person’s identity verified?
7. Were survivors searched for and protected?
8. Did an independent body investigate?
9. Is an effective remedy available?
10. Would the attacking state accept the same rule if another power used it against its nationals?

The sequence—executive designation, secret intelligence, lethal strike, posthumous label and no independent adjudication—is a **designation-to-kill chain**. The UN human-rights chief described the strikes as violating international human-rights law and called for investigations and for suspected offenders to be arrested and tried. Criticism occurred; effective external consequences sufficient to stop the practice did not.

This case precedes Venezuela in both time and logic. The maritime campaign began in September 2025. It demonstrated that exceptional labels, classified evidence and military killing could be normalised near Venezuela without effective deterrence. The 3 January intervention was a change of scale, not an event without a prehistory.

## 8. Sovereignty under coercion

After the maritime campaign, the United States’ 3 January 2026 intervention in Venezuela converted the threat into force against the state itself. It is not a prediction or metaphor. It is a documented military action. The Swiss Federal Department of Foreign Affairs concluded that the intervention and the capture of Nicolás Maduro violated the UN Charter’s prohibition on force, Venezuelan sovereignty and territorial integrity, the principle of non-intervention and Maduro’s personal immunity. It found no valid claim of self-defence, Security Council authorisation or Venezuelan consent.

That finding does not settle every political question inside Venezuela. A government can lack democratic legitimacy and still be protected by the international rules prohibiting another state from invading, abducting its officials or dictating its political order. **Legal attribution, democratic legitimacy and strategic autonomy are separate questions.**

Venezuela formally notified its withdrawal from the Rome Statute on 24 July 2026. The withdrawal takes effect on 24 July 2027. The official notice accused the International Criminal Court of selectivity, politicisation and “lawfare.” It followed Venezuela’s December 2025 legislative move toward withdrawal, the January US attack, continuing threats of further force and a July US campaign openly seeking to dismantle the Court’s reach and encourage withdrawals.

That sequence supports an inference of severely constrained autonomy. It does not, by itself, prove that Washington ordered the Venezuelan decision. The precise description is: **an official Venezuelan act taken in an environment of illegal force, credible continuing threats and open US hostility to the Court**. Under Article 127 of the Rome Statute, withdrawal does not erase obligations or proceedings concerning the period in which Venezuela was a party.

Later evidence shows how that constraint became an administrative structure. [US Executive Order 14373](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-for-the-good-of-the-american-and-venezuelan-people/) directs that certain revenues from Venezuelan natural resources remain in US Treasury accounts and that disbursements follow instructions from the secretary of state. A [BBC News Mundo investigation published on 10 August 2026](https://www.bbc.com/mundo/articles/ce8l6xj85ppo) also documented the simultaneous dependence of the interim government and parts of the opposition on Washington, and reported the expressions “suspended sovereignty” and “tutelage” used by Venezuelan specialists. Those expressions are attributed interpretations, not judicial findings. Combined with official US financial control and already demonstrated military capacity, they strengthen the political classification of **de facto tutelage** and the analytical classification of **constrained autonomy**; they do not, by themselves, prove belligerent occupation or US control over every act of the Venezuelan state.

## 9. A civilian operating protocol

This manual is not a personalised security instruction and does not promise that every precaution will work. It offers a repeatable way to reduce dependence on official narratives.

### Build an evidence file

- Save primary documents, dates and stable links.
- Record whether each proposition is a fact, allegation, finding, inference or forecast.
- Preserve copies of identity, residence, medical, financial and educational records securely and in more than one location.
- Keep a dated chronology; memory and headlines are poor archives.

### Map practical dependencies

- Identify which state controls your residence status, passport, banking access, family reunification and consular protection.
- Do not assume that legal rights guarantee rapid administrative relief.
- Understand sanctions and payment-channel exposure before a crisis blocks access.
- Separate public political speech from unnecessary disclosure of personal locations, routines or vulnerable relatives.

### Read institutional behaviour

- Compare policy instruments across cases, not only statements within one crisis.
- Look for veto points: unanimity rules, export licences, courts, alliance commitments and financial infrastructure.
- Treat humanitarian aid and condemnation as evidence of recognition, not automatically as evidence of deterrence.
- Ask which costs remain external to the decision-making state.

### Maintain intellectual discipline

- Do not adopt a government’s description merely because it is official.
- Do not weaken a documented case with claims the evidence cannot sustain.
- Correct errors publicly and preserve the distinction between accountability for officials and collective blame against peoples.
- Apply the same legal standard to allies, adversaries and one’s own government.

## Conclusion

The international system does not operate as its official language suggests. Law is neither imaginary nor self-executing. Powerful states invoke it, evade it, enforce it selectively and sometimes create institutional vocabulary to make coercion appear administrative. Smaller states can acquire leverage by being embedded in larger protective systems. European states can condemn destruction while funding its consequences and declining to raise its price. An intervention can be plainly unlawful without constituting occupation. A formal sovereign decision can be legally attributable to a government while being made under severe external pressure.

For civilians, realism is not cynicism. It is the practice of locating actual authority, dependency, instruments and costs before making decisions. The objective is not to surrender to power. It is to stop mistaking declarations for protection and to direct scrutiny toward the mechanisms through which power is exercised.

I began this work with fear and continued it with pain. Neither emotion proves a legal proposition. Both tell me which questions cannot responsibly be ignored. The task of this manual is to submit those questions to evidence without stripping them of their human meaning.
