# Case Study VI: Debt, Humanitarian Abandonment and Resource Control

## How Selective Austerity Exports Death and Turns Other People’s Resources into Instruments of Power

**By Arturo Sánchez Pineda**  
**31 August 2026**

*The sixth case study in **The Civilian Geopolitical Reality Map**—a framework for distinguishing fiscal facts, mortality forecasts, legal duties and the political selection of who is made to absorb a state’s costs.*

The United States’ gross national debt crossed US$40 trillion in August 2026. That is an extraordinary and consequential number. It does not, by itself, explain why a government dismantles humanitarian programmes, preserves far more expensive instruments of coercion or assumes control over revenues generated by another country’s natural resources. A balance-sheet constraint is real; the selection of sacrifice is political.

I am interested in that selection. People outside the United States did not vote for its tax policy, military expenditure, reconciliation legislation or accumulated interest burden. Yet they can lose food, antiretroviral treatment, prenatal care, clean water or protection when Washington chooses them as the least costly place to cut. The budget saving remains in the United States. The mortality risk is exported.

This study therefore separates five propositions that rhetoric too easily collapses. The debt is a verified fiscal fact. The dismantling of USAID and contraction of UN-connected programmes are documented policy and operational facts. The millions of additional deaths are modelled counterfactual forecasts, not a completed global body count. The use of Venezuelan resource revenue to service US debt remains unsupported by transaction evidence. A different use of those resources is now visible: the administration is presenting claimed control over Venezuelan oil as a promise of strategic supply and lower prices while the Iran war imposes an electoral energy cost.

## The number and the mechanism

The [Associated Press reported on 19 August 2026](https://apnews.com/article/a27a8d3651ff810b25c610d3e1b6259d), using Treasury data, that total gross federal debt had passed US$40 trillion. Gross debt combines debt held by the public with intragovernmental holdings. It is not the same measure as debt held by the public, which the [Congressional Budget Office](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105) projected at 101 per cent of GDP in 2026 and 120 per cent in 2036.

CBO projected a US$1.9 trillion federal deficit for fiscal year 2026, more than US$1 trillion in net interest outlays and a rising interest burden over the following decade. Its account identifies growth in Social Security, Medicare and net interest, together with major changes produced by the 2025 reconciliation act, tariffs and immigration actions. It does not identify humanitarian assistance as the cause of the fiscal trajectory.

Debt creates pressure, arguments and political opportunity. It does not issue instructions. Nothing in the number US$40 trillion determines that a mobile health team in Afghanistan should close before a weapons programme, a tax preference or another domestic expenditure changes. **Debt produces a field of austerity politics; governments decide who is sacrificed within it.**

## A dismantled civilian infrastructure

The dismantling was administrative before it was statistical. [Executive Order 14169](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/reevaluating-and-realigning-united-states-foreign-aid/) ordered a pause and review of foreign development assistance in January 2025. The non-partisan [Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10261) records that Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced the termination of 83 per cent of USAID-managed programmes in March and the transfer of the remaining programmes to the State Department. The 83 per cent figure describes programmes, not a measured 83 per cent reduction in every budget line or every country.

These were not interchangeable grants that could be switched off without destroying capacity. Humanitarian and development systems are networks of trained workers, laboratories, supply contracts, vaccination routes, data systems, local partners and relationships of trust. A funding stop can dismiss the people and dissolve the logistics before a court, legislature or successor donor can reverse it.

The UN Secretary-General described immediate effects of the US cuts in February 2025: more than nine million people in Afghanistan losing health and protection services, programmes suspended in north-east Syria, cash assistance suspended in parts of Ukraine, exhausted support in South Sudan and interruptions to programmes against HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria and cholera. These were not forecasts from a political opponent. They were operational consequences reported by the institutions running the programmes.

The contraction then spread through the larger humanitarian system and was compounded by cuts from other donors. In July 2025, the [World Food Programme](https://www.wfp.org/publications/food-security-impact-reduction-wfp-funding) projected resources 34 per cent below 2024 and estimated that reduced emergency food assistance could affect 16.7 million people, including three million already in emergency food insecurity. [UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/us/news/briefing-notes/unhcr-funding-cuts-threaten-health-nearly-13-million-displaced-people) warned that 12.8 million displaced people, including 6.3 million children, could be left without life-saving health interventions in 2025. These figures describe exposure and loss of service; they are not themselves death counts.

## What the mortality numbers do—and do not—prove

A peer-reviewed 2025 study in *The Lancet* estimated that USAID funding was associated with 91.8 million deaths prevented between 2001 and 2021, including 30.4 million among children younger than five. Under its defunding scenario, the model projected approximately 14.1 million additional deaths from 2025 through 2030, including 4.5 million children younger than five.

A broader 2026 study in *The Lancet Global Health* examined official development assistance from all major donors across 93 low- and middle-income countries. It projected 9.4 million additional deaths through 2030 if recent downward trends continued and 22.6 million under its severe-defunding scenario, including respectively 2.5 million and 5.4 million children younger than five.

Those numbers must be described as forecasts generated by counterfactual models. They compare expected mortality under different funding trajectories. They are not millions of individually certified deaths already attributed to a single administrative act. Their uncertainty intervals, assumptions and dependence on future policy are part of the result.

That qualification does not make the risk imaginary. [Associated Press reporting from Nepal in August 2026](https://apnews.com/article/43fb5e8aff8a4f65b7f18781455c3f91) documented the concrete mechanism: discontinued reproductive-health and nutrition outreach, lost follow-up for high-risk pregnancies, weakened data collection, and health workers and officials reporting increased complications and deaths. The reporting does not establish a global causal count or prove that a specific programme would have prevented every individual death. It shows how the statistical risk becomes a person, a pregnancy, an untreated condition and then an absence in a family.

The accurate conclusion is therefore already severe. Millions of deaths have not been observed and individually audited as the result of the cuts. Peer-reviewed models forecast millions of additional deaths if the cuts persist, while field reporting and UN operational records show that the life-preserving mechanisms assumed by those models have in fact been removed.

## When is cutting aid contrary to international law?

The moral category is broader than the legal one. International law does not impose a general obligation on the United States to finance every foreign programme or every voluntary appeal by a UN agency. Ending a voluntary contribution can be cruel, reckless and foreseeably lethal without automatically constituting a separate internationally wrongful act.

Specific duties change the analysis. Assessed contributions to the United Nations are apportioned under Article 17 of the UN Charter and are not the same as voluntary humanitarian donations. In armed conflict, parties and relevant transit states must allow and facilitate rapid and unimpeded passage of impartial humanitarian relief, subject to the defined controls recognised by international humanitarian law; withdrawing donor money is not legally identical to blocking an available relief convoy. Under the Genocide Convention, the duty to prevent arises when a state knows or should know of a serious risk and requires use of the means reasonably available to it according to its capacity to influence. Financial and military leverage can therefore matter legally in a genocide-risk situation even though no universal duty exists to fund every aid programme.

The distinction prevents two errors. It prevents governments from using the absence of a universal funding duty to describe foreseeable mass death as an accident. It also prevents me from declaring every budget cut a war crime without identifying an armed conflict, the actor’s legal role, the specific duty and the conduct that breaches it.

The sharper question is often cumulative. A state may withdraw relief while continuing weapons, intelligence, diplomatic protection or economic support to an actor creating the humanitarian need. The legal analysis then cannot examine the missing food and the supplied coercive capacity as if they belonged to unrelated worlds. Each contribution, omission, knowledge standard and causal link still has to be proved, but the full relationship is the object to investigate.

## Debt and the selection of sacrifice

Foreign aid is politically vulnerable because many of the people who live or die through it have no vote in the donor state. The costs of a cut are distant, delayed, dispersed across countries and often recorded in health statistics rather than on a battlefield. The domestic political benefit—an announcement of savings, ideological control or administrative destruction—is immediate.

That asymmetry explains why an expenditure can be fiscally small relative to a US$40 trillion stock of gross debt and still be existential for the person who loses a ration or medicine. Eliminating humanitarian capacity does not solve the structural drivers identified by CBO. It demonstrates which lives the government treats as available for an inexpensive performance of austerity.

I call this **fiscal externalisation**: a state retains the political benefit of a budget choice while transferring its human cost to people outside the electorate and outside the institutions able to reverse the choice. The classification is an analytical inference, not a claim that debt mechanically caused the cuts or that every official desired the resulting deaths. The policy is condemnable without inventing a secret motive. Foreseeability, choice and refusal to reverse course are enough to establish responsibility in political and moral analysis; legal responsibility requires the additional rule-specific tests above.

## Venezuela: claimed control becomes an electoral energy instrument

[Executive Order 14373](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-for-the-good-of-the-american-and-venezuelan-people/) established one form of control in January: specified Venezuelan resource revenues are held in designated US Treasury accounts, transfer or withdrawal requires US authorisation and the secretary of state controls disbursement instructions. The order says that the money remains Venezuelan sovereign property. That distinction between ownership and control matters again in the agreement announced on 28 August.

Trump described that agreement as the largest oil deal in history and claimed US majority control over 65 billion barrels—more than the United States’ own proved crude reserves. Rubio advertised it as a route to lower American gasoline prices. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/venezuela-oil-trump-deal-unknown-questions-reserves-c229bc39b6e1a3d5dd16f7f9e67fef3f) reported, attributing the terms to an unnamed US official, that the United States would receive 55 per cent of the new company’s effective output through an ownership stake and at-cost purchasing rights, with purchased oil directed to the Strategic Petroleum Reserve and military use. Venezuela announced 17 fields, 65 billion barrels of potential, US$100 billion in investment and more than US$209 billion in projected taxes and royalties. Rodríguez said the arrangement lasted 25 years and preserved Venezuelan ownership; the US source told AP that development rights lasted 100 years. No agreement text had been published by 31 August.

The domestic political timing is not conjecture. AP placed the announcement amid mounting pressure over fuel prices, six months into the Iran war and months before the November elections. The [US Energy Information Administration](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/?pubDate=20260830) recorded regular gasoline at US$4.085 per gallon on 24 August—US$0.938 above the price one year earlier—and diesel at US$5.652, up US$1.944. The [International Energy Agency’s August report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-august-2026) documented the mechanism behind the wider shock: the continuing closure of the Strait of Hormuz, 8.3 million barrels per day of Gulf output still shut in, observed inventories 410 million barrels lower since the war began, and North Sea Dated ending July at US$96.80 per barrel.

Those facts do not prove that the Iran war caused every cent of the US pump-price increase. They do establish that the war constrained Gulf supply and kept oil and fuel prices elevated while the administration advertised Venezuelan reserves as relief. They also expose a mismatch in time. AP’s energy specialists said Venezuela’s degraded infrastructure would require years and billions of dollars before producing a substantial increase. The electorate receives the image of control now; any material supply effect arrives later, if the unpublished arrangement survives its legal, political, financial and engineering risks.

The strategic context is similarly attributable rather than invented. [James M. Lindsay of the Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/six-months-into-the-iran-war-the-united-states-has-no-good-options) wrote that the operation failed to produce Trump’s promised quick victory: after six months Iran had neither collapsed nor capitulated, tanker traffic through Hormuz remained far below pre-war levels and the United States had no good option to compel its demands. [Elliott Abrams](https://www.cfr.org/articles/trumps-iran-negotiations-trade-the-art-of-the-deal-for-the-art-of-concealing-defeat)—Trump’s former special representative for Iran and Venezuela—argued that the president wanted oil to flow and prices to fall before the congressional elections, and that an arrangement leaving Iran in control of Hormuz would use negotiation to conceal defeat. “Strategic defeat” is their assessment, not an official adjudicated fact. The failed timetable, unmet objectives, constrained waterway and sustained energy cost are the observable record supporting it.

The political operation can therefore be named without claiming access to Trump’s private mind. The administration converts Venezuelan oil in the ground into “American” reserve power, promises cheaper fuel and presents domination abroad as competence at home. It is reasonable to infer that the announcement functions as electoral compensation for—and an attempt to cover—the Iran war’s strategic and price failure. The record does not show that this was the agreement’s sole motive, that Venezuelan production can lower prices before the election or that the deal will be implemented as announced.

The debt boundary remains, but it is no longer the centre of the map. The executive order and new announcement still do not show Venezuelan revenue entering the US general fund or paying Treasury creditors. **There is no evidence that these resources are servicing US federal debt.** There is now evidence that claimed control over them is being used as an immediate domestic political asset and that the announced structure reaches production, at-cost purchase and US strategic supply.

Venezuelans should demand two public ledgers. The first is financial: volumes, prices, buyers, accounts, interest, fees, taxes, royalties, beneficiaries and independent audit. The second is political and productive: the complete agreement, corporate owners, voting rights, duration, field licences, production shares, at-cost purchase formula, investment obligations, military or reserve deliveries and every Venezuelan approval. A sovereignty claim that cannot survive publication is not accountability.

## The civilian audit

For any future claim that debt requires humanitarian cuts or external resource control, ask:

1. Which debt measure is being used: gross debt, debt held by the public, annual deficit or interest cost?
2. Which law or budget decision created the claimed constraint?
3. Which expenditures, tax choices and military commitments were preserved?
4. Are the human consequences observed, modelled or merely asserted?
5. Is the funding voluntary, assessed under a treaty system or connected to a specific humanitarian-law duty?
6. Who owns the controlled resource, who holds it and who authorises each disbursement?
7. What evidence would distinguish custody, coercive administration, diversion and confiscation?
8. Who receives the fiscal benefit and who has been made to absorb the bodily cost?
9. Is a promise of future supply being presented as if it changed today’s prices, reserves or military position?
10. Which asserted “control” consists of ownership, voting rights, field licences, purchasing options, physical custody or political rhetoric?

This audit converts outrage into a reproducible inquiry. It does not make the conclusion gentle. It makes the conclusion harder to evade.

## Conclusion

Debt does not close a clinic. A government does. Debt does not terminate a food route, dismiss a health worker or decide that a foreign child is an acceptable saving. Officials make those choices within a fiscal environment whose other components they also shape.

The United States’ fiscal position is serious. So is the humanitarian system it chose to break. The first fact cannot be used to erase the second, and a programme’s location outside US territory does not make its foreseeable human consequences unreal. The current evidence supports a precise accusation: the administration dismantled life-preserving capacity; UN agencies recorded immediate service losses; peer-reviewed models forecast mortality in the millions if defunding persists; and real communities are already showing the mechanisms by which those deaths occur.

The Venezuela finding has changed in an important way. US control is no longer documented only through custody of specified Treasury-held revenue. The two governments announced a productive arrangement, while Washington claimed majority control, at-cost access, replenishment of strategic reserves, military supply and lower gasoline prices. There is still no evidence that Venezuelan resources are paying US federal debt. But there is now direct evidence that their claimed control is being used to reassure the US electorate during an Iran war that failed to deliver the quick victory promised and continued to elevate energy costs.

Fiscal externalisation is one manifestation of the pattern, not the whole pattern. The broader problem is that powerful states can retain the electoral, military or economic benefit of their choices while transferring their costs to people with little capacity to vote, resist, demand the contract or enforce the law. In this episode, Washington claims the victory and the future barrels now; Venezuelans carry the sovereignty, legal and production risk; and the story is sold before the oil capable of changing the market exists.

That is the purpose of this map: not to make power sound respectable, and not to make anger substitute for proof, but to know exactly what has happened, what may happen next and which document would prove that the line has been crossed.
